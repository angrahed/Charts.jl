# Implementations of the grammar of graphics: ggplot2's architecture, its extension ecosystem, user pain points, and other grammar libraries (outside Julia and the Vega family)

Research date: 11–12 October 2026. Scope excludes Vega/Vega-Lite/Altair, D3, ECharts, Highcharts and Plotly.js themselves (covered elsewhere) and all Julia packages. "Local check" means I ran the code against ggplot2 4.0.3 (the current CRAN release) on this machine. Those results are primary evidence, with the CRAN page as the reference: https://cran.r-project.org/package=ggplot2

## 1. ggplot2 internals: ggproto, the build→gtable pipeline, component representation, themes, the 3.5.0 guide rewrite, and 4.0 (S7, ink/paper, palettes from theme)

### Takeaway
ggplot2 is a lazy, immutable plot *specification* (data, layers, scales, coord, facet, theme, guides) that turns into a picture only when printed. This happens in two stages. `ggplot_build()` computes the data: stats, positions and scale training. `ggplot_gtable()` turns that data into a grid/gtable layout of grobs. ggplot2 itself draws nothing. Its history shows that extensibility, theming and validation were retrofitted rather than designed in: guides moved onto ggproto only in 3.5.0 (Feb 2024), layer defaults moved into the theme only in 4.0.0 (Sept 2025, eight years after the request), and S7 replaced S3 for the plot, theme, element, aes and labels classes in 4.0.0, which broke dozens of reverse dependencies. As of October 2026 the current release is 4.0.3 (22 April 2026), and no 4.1 has been announced.

### Cited Findings

**Release state (as of Oct 2026)**
- 4.0.3 (CRAN, 2026-04-22) is the newest release. The changelog lists no newer development entries. 4.0.3 is a hotfix that also adds a `quantile.type` parameter to `geom_boxplot()/stat_boxplot()` "enabling parity with SAS boxplots out-of-the-box". 4.0.2 (2026-02-03) was an rlang interoperability hotfix for `make_constructor()`. 4.0.1 fixed regressions in `geom_area()` and `position_stack(vjust)` and the palette fallback. — [ggplot2 changelog](https://ggplot2.tidyverse.org/news/index.html); [RDocumentation 4.0.2](https://www.rdocumentation.org/packages/ggplot2/versions/4.0.2)
- A Rust re-implementation (ggplot-rs, 2026) validates its binning, density, stacking, QQ/ECDF, LOESS and tick placement against "R ggplot2 4.0.3's `ggplot_build()` output", which shows 4.0.3 is the reference version in 2026. — [ggplot-rs README](https://github.com/sipemu/ggplot-rs)

**Object model: a plot is a specification, rendered lazily**
- "Rendering occurs only when the object is printed, not during construction", so plots can be modified incrementally without recomputation. — [ggplot2 book, Internals](https://ggplot2-book.org/internals.html)
- **ggproto** is "a minimal object-oriented system created specifically for ggplot2 when transitioning from proto proved problematic and switching to R6/S4 proved difficult". It has reference semantics with inheritance and is defined as `ggproto("Name", Parent, fields_and_methods)`. Methods get an implicit `self`. — [Internals](https://ggplot2-book.org/internals.html)
- Design constraints the book states explicitly: "ggproto classes in ggplot2 are assumed to be stateless", because state changes would leak across every instance that shares a parent. Extension authors should subclass existing classes: "it is neither expected nor advised to create entirely new ggproto classes in your extensions." — [Internals](https://ggplot2-book.org/internals.html)
- Local check (ggplot2 4.0.3): the namespace holds 133 ggproto objects, broken down as 47 `Geom*`, 32 `Stat*`, 13 `Guide*`, 12 `Scale*`, 11 `Coord*`, 9 `Position*`, 4 `Facet*`, 2 `Layer*`, 1 `Layout` and 2 others. The package exports 643 objects, including 53 `geom_*`, 36 `stat_*`, 131 `scale_*` and 25 `theme_*` functions. — local check; [CRAN ggplot2](https://cran.r-project.org/package=ggplot2)
- Local check: after 4.0, `class(ggplot())` is `ggplot2::ggplot, ggplot, ggplot2::gg, S7_object, gg`. `theme()` and `aes()` (class `ggplot2::mapping`/`uneval`) are also S7. Layers (`LayerInstance`/`ggproto`) and scales (`ScaleContinuousPosition … ggproto`) are still ggproto. So the user-facing spec objects are S7, and the compute machinery is still ggproto. — local check; consistent with the S7 plan in [issue #6352](https://github.com/tidyverse/ggplot2/issues/6352)

**`ggplot_build()` (data pipeline), in order**
- The steps, per the book: resolve the layer data (explicit, inherited or function), then pass it through the facet layout, which adds a `PANEL` column. Next compute the aesthetics from `aes()` and derive `group` from the interaction of the discrete aesthetics. Then come scale transformation (`trans` applied *before* stats), position-scale mapping (out-of-bounds handling, NA removal, discrete→integer), and the statistical computation, split by PANEL and group. After that the stat-computed aesthetics are mapped (`after_stat()`), the geom runs its setup (for example x+width → xmin/xmax), positions are adjusted (dodge/stack/jitter), and the position scales are **reset and retrained**. Finally the non-position scales are trained and mapped, geom defaults are added, and `finish_data()` hooks run. The result is a `ggplot_built` object (computed layer data plus a `Layout` with trained coords and facets, plus the plot with trained scales). — [Internals](https://ggplot2-book.org/internals.html)
- The `Layout` object coordinates the coord and the facet, determines the panel structure and manages `PANEL` throughout. — [Internals](https://ggplot2-book.org/internals.html)

**`ggplot_gtable()` (render pipeline)**
- Each layer becomes grobs, split by PANEL and then by group, and the coord normalises positions. The facet then assembles the panels and adds strips, backgrounds, gridlines and axes from the theme. Guides are trained per scale, compatible guides are **merged**, and key glyphs are requested from each contributing layer. The legend gtable is then styled and positioned by `legend.position`. Titles, subtitle, caption, tag, background and margins are added last. "ggplot2 creates a gtable … but performs no drawing itself", so drawing is delegated to grid and the active device. — [Internals](https://ggplot2-book.org/internals.html)
- On performance, the maintainer's profiling vignette measures only up to gtable construction, because "Any performance problems in rendering is likely due to grid and the device, more than ggplot2". Past fixes include avoiding `factor()` in `scale_apply` (an issue "only really appeared when plotting huge datasets (>1e6 rows)"), caching `grid::descentDetails()` for titles ("the absolute biggest offender was the construction of titles"), replacing `data.frame()` and `modifyList()` with faster internals, and treating data as lists during position transformation. — [Profiling vignette (T. L. Pedersen)](https://ggplot2.tidyverse.org/articles/profiling.html) ([source](https://github.com/tidyverse/ggplot2/blob/main/vignettes/profiling.qmd))

**Theme system and element inheritance**
- There are four parts: theme elements, element functions (`element_text/line/rect/blank`), `theme()`, and complete themes. Inheritance works like this: "any properties that you don't explicitly set in `axis.text.x` and `axis.text.y` will be inherited from `axis.text`." — [ggplot2 book, Themes](https://ggplot2-book.org/themes.html)
- Local check: `get_element_tree()` lists **160 theme elements**, and `theme_grey()` sets 144 of them. `theme_grey()` now has the arguments `base_size = 11`, `base_family`, `header_family`, `base_line_size`, `base_rect_size`, `ink = "black"`, `paper = "white"` and `accent = "#3366FF"`. — local check
- 4.0 added shortcut functions to reduce theme verbosity: `theme_sub_axis()`, `theme_sub_axis_x/y()`, `theme_sub_axis_bottom/top/left/right()`, `theme_sub_legend()`, `theme_sub_panel()`, `theme_sub_plot()` and `theme_sub_strip()`. It also added CSS-like `margin_auto()` and `margin_part()`, new root elements `spacing` and `margins` (combined with `rel()`), and `panel.widths`/`panel.heights` for fixing panel sizes. — [ggplot2 4.0.0 post](https://tidyverse.org/blog/2025/09/ggplot2-4-0-0/)

**ggplot2 4.0: theme-driven layer defaults (ink/paper/accent, element_geom, palettes from theme)**
- Themes now carry layer defaults that were previously treated as "non-data-driven choices". `ink` is the foreground (lines, points, text), `paper` the background, and `accent` is used by geoms such as `geom_smooth()`, `geom_contour()` and `geom_quantile()`. Panel backgrounds mix paper and ink. `element_geom()` sets layer defaults in the theme and `from_theme()` reads theme values inside mappings, e.g. `aes(colour = from_theme(scales::col_darker(accent, 20)))`. The release also pairs `linewidth/linetype` (lines that represent data) with `borderwidth/bordertype` (separating borders). — [4.0.0 post](https://tidyverse.org/blog/2025/09/ggplot2-4-0-0/); [ggplot2 styling post](https://tidyverse.org/blog/2025/10/ggplot2-styling/)
- Palettes in the theme follow the pattern `palette.{aesthetic}.{discrete|continuous}`, e.g. `palette.shape.discrete = c("plus","triangle","diamond")`. Scales become "coordinated" with the theme. — [styling post](https://tidyverse.org/blog/2025/10/ggplot2-styling/)
- A four-quadrant customisation hierarchy: one layer in one plot uses static aesthetics in the layer; one layer type in all plots uses `update_theme(geom.point = element_geom(...))`; all layers in one plot use `theme(geom = element_geom(...))`; all layers in all plots use `set_theme()` or `update_theme(geom = ...)`. — [styling post](https://tidyverse.org/blog/2025/10/ggplot2-styling/)
- The request to put geom defaults in the theme ("Geoms and scales: default settings in theme", #2239) dates from **2017-08** and is among the most-reacted ggplot2 issues. — [GitHub #2239](https://github.com/tidyverse/ggplot2/issues/2239); [reaction-sorted issue search](https://github.com/tidyverse/ggplot2/issues?q=is%3Aissue+sort%3Areactions-%2B1-desc)
- scales 1.4.0 (Apr 2025) laid the groundwork. It added colour manipulation functions (`col_shift()`, `col_lighter()`, `col_darker()`, `col_saturate()`, `col_mix()`), and palettes became classed objects (`pal_discrete`/`pal_continuous`) "so metadata about the palette [can] be stored and used downstream". — [scales 1.4.0 post](https://tidyverse.org/blog/2025/04/scales-1-4-0/)

**Other 4.0 changes relevant to a new design**
- Labels are resolved through an explicit precedence chain, lowest to highest: the `aes()` expression, then a `labs(dictionary=)` entry, then the column's `label` attribute, then `labs(<aes>=)`, then the scale `name`, then the guide `title`. Functions are allowed at each level. — [4.0.0 post](https://tidyverse.org/blog/2025/09/ggplot2-4-0-0/)
- `layer(layout=)` controls how a layer's data is assigned to facet panels (`NULL`, `"fixed"`, an integer panel, or `"fixed_cols"`/`"fixed_rows"`). `facet_wrap(dir=)` unifies eight fill orders (`"lt"`, `"tl"`, …). Position adjustments declare their own aesthetics (`nudge_x` and `nudge_y` become aesthetics, and `position_dodge()` gains an `order` aesthetic). `stat_manual()` takes an arbitrary data-frame→data-frame function, and `stat_connect()` generalises step lines. A `reverse` argument replaces transform-based axis flipping. Discrete scales gain `palette` for position, `continuous.limits` and `minor_breaks`, and secondary axes on discrete scales via `dup_axis()`. — [4.0.0 post](https://tidyverse.org/blog/2025/09/ggplot2-4-0-0/)
- For extension authors there are `make_constructor()` (it auto-generates `geom_*()`/`stat_*()` constructors), `gg_par()` (which handles unit conversion, e.g. linewidth mm→pt), `complete_theme()`, and new `point`/`polygon` theme elements. — [4.0.0 post](https://tidyverse.org/blog/2025/09/ggplot2-4-0-0/)

**S7 migration: rationale and fallout**
- The rationale was that "There are some issues with the S3 OOP system that cannot be resolved in a satisfying way". The S7 targets were the ggplot, theme and element classes, the `uneval` class (from `aes()`) and the labels class (from `labs()`). The maintainers anticipated "reverse dependency failures". — [issue #6352](https://github.com/tidyverse/ggplot2/issues/6352)
- The concrete S3 problems were `+` dispatch. Packages that subclass `gg` (patchwork, GGally) could not control `foo_obj + gg_obj`, the left-hand side ([#3986](https://github.com/tidyverse/ggplot2/issues/3986)), and `ggplot() + list(...)` was not equivalent to chained `+` for gg subclasses ([#5536](https://github.com/tidyverse/ggplot2/issues/5536)). In 4.0, `update_ggplot()` (the successor to `ggplot_add()`) uses S7 double dispatch on both operands. Invalid inputs now fail early; for example `element_text(hjust = "foo")` errors. `$` access still works, but `@` is the future. — [4.0.0 post](https://tidyverse.org/blog/2025/09/ggplot2-4-0-0/)
- Fallout: in revdep checks, about 64 packages broke on the change to S7 labels and the deferral of default labels to build time, and about 8 broke on S7's stricter property types. — [#6505](https://github.com/tidyverse/ggplot2/issues/6505), [#6507](https://github.com/tidyverse/ggplot2/issues/6507). Users also hit `Incompatible methods ("Ops.S7_object", "+.gg") for "+"` errors after upgrading. — [Posit Community thread](https://forum.posit.co/t/incompatible-methods-ops-s7-object-gg-for/207470). Bioconductor's twice-yearly releases made the transition harder. — [R-bloggers, Bioconductor and ggplot2 4.0.0](https://www.r-bloggers.com/2025/07/bioconductor-and-ggplot2-4-0-0-whats-changing-and-how-to-prepare/)

**Guide-system rewrite (3.5.0, Feb 2024)**
- Guides were "the only major system in ggplot2 still clinging to the S3 system". The rewrite to ggproto made guides an official extension point, and the "Extending ggplot2" vignette gained a section on writing a guide. — [ggplot2 3.5.0 post](https://www.tidyverse.org/blog/2024/02/ggplot2-3-5-0/)
- New guides: `guide_axis_stack()`, `guide_axis_theta()`, `guide_axis_logticks()` and `guide_custom()`. `coord_radial()` superseded `coord_polar()`, and pattern and gradient fills arrived. — [changelog](https://ggplot2.tidyverse.org/news/index.html)
- Legend changes in 3.5.0: guide styling moved to `theme()` (giving "only one way to style guides", with per-guide `theme` arguments). Legends now show keys only for layers that actually contain the relevant values. Multiple legend positions and an `"inside"` position arrived (`legend.position.inside`, `legend.justification.{position}`). The spacing system was redesigned (`legend.spacing` vs `legend.key.spacing`), legend keys can stretch with `"null"` units, `guide_custom()` adds arbitrary grobs, and the legend title respects `hjust`. — [3.5.0 legends post](https://www.tidyverse.org/blog/2024/02/ggplot2-3-5-0-legends/)
- Since 3.5.0, "Plot scales now ignore `AsIs` objects constructed with `I(x)`, instead of invoking the identity scale … it makes it easy to specify relative positions" (#5142). In 4.0.0, custom and raster annotations "can use AsIs variables for relative placement". Local check: `annotate("text", x = I(0.05), y = I(0.95), …)` keeps x=0.05, y=0.95 as panel-relative positions through the build. — [changelog, 3.5.0 and 4.0.0](https://ggplot2.tidyverse.org/news/index.html); [3.5.0 post](https://www.tidyverse.org/blog/2024/02/ggplot2-3-5-0/); local check
- Plot titles have been left-aligned by default since 2.2.0 (2016): "The main plot title is now left-aligned to better work better with a subtitle." — [changelog, 2.2.0](https://ggplot2.tidyverse.org/news/index.html)

### Inferences
- The two-stage split (compute data, then lay out grobs) is ggplot2's most reusable idea: plotly's `ggplotly()`, ggiraph and the Rust ggplot-rs validation harness all consume `ggplot_build()` output. A new library should expose an equivalent, stable and documented "built plot" intermediate representation (trained scales, per-layer computed data, layout) as public API, not internals. That also serves Pu & Kay's call for transparency (§3).
- The theme's 160 elements, with string-keyed dot-path inheritance and a separate per-layer default system that was only unified in 2025, are a cautionary tale. A new design should have a single, typed style tree from day one, covering both non-data elements and layer/mark defaults, with semantic roles (ink, paper, accent, palettes) at the root and derived values computed from them.
- ggplot2 needed two retrofits, ggproto guides (2024) and S7 validation and double dispatch (2025), to get extensibility and type checking. Julia's multiple dispatch and parametric types give both natively, so the extension points (Mark, Stat, Position, Scale, Coord, Facet, Guide, Theme element) should be abstract types with documented method contracts from the first release.
- Per-layer position adjustments with their own aesthetics, `layer(layout=)`, and label dictionaries are ideas worth copying directly into a new core.

## 2. The ggplot2 extension ecosystem: which gaps do the major extensions fill, and which belong in a new core?

### Takeaway
There are 167 registered ggplot2 extensions. The most-used ones fill a small number of recurring gaps: multi-plot composition with shared legends and axes, label collision avoidance and direct labelling, rich text, emphasis and highlighting, richer faceting and panel sizing, fonts and device-independent sizing, interactivity, animation, and domain grammars (uncertainty, time). Several have slowly been pulled into ggplot2 or are slated to be: legend placement, axis guides, panel sizes, label dictionaries, and marquee's rich text. Interactivity has stayed outside, and the post-hoc conversion approach (ggplotly) is lossy. For a new library, composition, repel and direct labelling, rich text, highlighting, interaction aesthetics, fonts and output scaling, and nested facets with per-panel scales should be core.

### Cited Findings
- **Gallery size:** the official gallery lists **167 registered extensions**, spanning interactivity (ggiraph, esquisse), layout (patchwork, cowplot, geofacet, ggalign), labels and text (ggrepel, ggtext, geomtextpath, ggfittext), themes (ggthemes, hrbrthemes, ggdark), animation (gganimate) and facets (ggragged). — [ggplot2 extensions gallery](https://exts.ggplot2.tidyverse.org/gallery/)
- **patchwork (Pedersen)** composes plots with arithmetic operators. Version 1.2.0 (Jan 2024) added `plot_layout(axes = "collect", axis_titles = "collect")` to remove duplicated axes and titles, alongside `guides = "collect"|"keep"|"auto"` for nesting-aware legend collection. — [patchwork 1.2.0 post](https://www.data-imaginist.com/posts/2024-01-05-patchwork-1-2-0/); [patchwork changelog](https://patchwork.data-imaginist.com/news/index.html)
- **cowplot (Wilke)** provides `plot_grid()`, `align_plots()`, `draw_plot()`/`draw_*` annotation on top of plots, `get_legend()`, and publication themes (`theme_cowplot()`, `theme_minimal_grid()`). Version 1.0.0 (2019-07-11): "The default ggplot2 theme is no longer changed upon loading". — [cowplot NEWS](https://wilkelab.org/cowplot/news/index.html)
- **ggrepel** repels overlapping text labels (gallery description). Pu & Kay cite it as an example of an "aesthetic-space instruction" ("no overlap") that the core grammar lacks. — [gallery](https://exts.ggplot2.tidyverse.org/gallery/); [Pu & Kay 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3544548.3580837)
- **ggtext → marquee (rich text):** ggtext gives "improved text rendering support" (markdown/HTML in theme elements and labels). Pedersen's marquee (2024) is a CommonMark parser and grid renderer with `element_marquee()`, which "should behave similar to `element_text()`", and its two ggplot2 helpers are intended to "eventually move … into ggplot2 proper". The GitHub request "Use markdown for italic/bold in titles" (#2743, 2018) is among the most-reacted issues. — [gallery](https://exts.ggplot2.tidyverse.org/gallery/); [marquee 0.1.0 post](https://tidyverse.org/blog/2024/05/marquee-0-1-0/); [marquee reference](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/marquee/refman/marquee.html); [#2743](https://github.com/tidyverse/ggplot2/issues/2743)
- **ggh4x (van den Brand, now a ggplot2 maintainer)** adds nested facets with spanning strips, manual facet layouts, themed strips, per-panel position scales ("adjusting the position scales on a per-panel basis"), panel sizes free of the global `aspect.ratio`, multiple colour scales in one plot, and nested, string and dendrogram axis guides. The docs point to overlapping packages (facetscales, egg, lemon, ggnewscale). — [ggh4x](https://teunbrand.github.io/ggh4x/). ggplot2 4.0 added `panel.widths`/`panel.heights`, which overlaps ggh4x's panel sizing. — [4.0.0 post](https://tidyverse.org/blog/2025/09/ggplot2-4-0-0/)
- **ggiraph** makes plots interactive through `*_interactive` geoms and the aesthetics `tooltip`, `data_id` and `onclick`, rendered by `girafe()` to SVG via the `dsvg` device. Scales, facets, guides and theme elements can also be interactive, `data_id` drives hover effects and Shiny selection, and it works with patchwork and cowplot. — [ggiraph book](https://www.ardata.fr/ggiraph-book/intro.html)
- **plotly::ggplotly** "translates each ggplot2 layer into one or more plotly.js traces" and is "forced to make a number of assumptions about trace attribute values that may or may not be appropriate". The root cause the author gives is that "ggplot2 doesn't provide an API for interactive features", so fixes come after the fact via `style()`, `layout()`, `config()` and the `layerData`/`originalData` arguments. — [Sievert, *Interactive web-based data visualization with R, plotly, and shiny*, ch. "Improving ggplotly"](https://plotly-r.com/improving-ggplotly.html)
- **animint (academic precedent for grammar-level interactivity)** adds two aesthetics, `clickSelects` (a geom that changes a selection variable when clicked) and `showSelected` (a geom that shows only the data for the current selection), plus tooltip and hyperlink extensions. Its architecture has two parts: an R compiler that turns ggplots into a data and plot database, and an HTML/JavaScript (D3) renderer. — [Sievert et al. 2019, JCGS](https://doi.org/10.1080/10618600.2018.1513367) ([PDF](http://tdhock.github.io/assets/papers/Sievert2019jcgs.pdf)); animint2 is still on CRAN (Oct 2025) — [CRAN animint2](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/animint2/index.html)
- **gganimate** is billed as "A Grammar of Animated Graphics", extending the grammar with transition, view, shadow and enter/exit components. — [gganimate reference](https://gganimate.com/reference/gganimate-package.html)
- **ggdist (Kay)** maps "functions of distributions (e.g. densities, CDFs, quantiles) onto aesthetics" via a `slab` geometry with an orientation-aware `thickness` aesthetic. It needs custom scale sharing (`scale_thickness_shared`) because "faceting doesn't cut it", and averages about 14,000 downloads a month. — [Kay 2024, IEEE TVCG](https://doi.org/10.1109/TVCG.2023.3327195) ([PDF](https://mucollective.northwestern.edu/files/2023-ggdist.pdf))
- **ggdibbler (2026)** argues that visualisations of random-variable inputs should obey the continuous mapping theorem, and implements "a complete integration of uncertainty into the grammar of graphics" as a ggplot2 extension. — [Mason et al. 2026, arXiv:2606.24217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24217)
- **ggtime (2025)** is a grammar of temporal graphics adding Geometry, Scale, Position and Coordinate extensions for linear, cyclical and quasi-cyclical granularities, irregular durations, and alignment across granularities and time zones. Its motivation is that bespoke helper functions dominate "due to the absence of flexible general tools that respect temporal semantics". — [Huang et al. 2025, arXiv:2510.25656](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25656)
- **systemfonts / textshaping / ragg / svglite (Pedersen)**: in R graphics, "it is up to the graphics device to find the appropriate font file … and shape the text", and problems range from not finding installed fonts to no fallback and no right-to-left support. R exposes only plain/bold/italic/bold-italic ("there's no way to select Futura Condensed Extra Bold"). systemfonts and textshaping provide lookup, fallback, registration and shaping. The recommendations are ragg for raster and svglite for SVG, `get_from_google_fonts()`, `require_font()` and `register_variant()`. PDF remains "a big hole". — [Fonts in R (May 2025)](https://tidyverse.org/blog/2025/05/fonts-in-r/); [Text rendering updates (Jan 2025)](https://tidyverse.org/blog/2025/01/text-rendering-updates/)
- **ragg's `scaling` argument** is "a multiplier that is applied to all absolute sizes, without interfering with the encoded dimensions of the output". It addresses the problem that, when output size grows, "relative sizes expand along with the output size, the absolute sizes does not". — [Taking control of plot scaling (2020)](https://www.tidyverse.org/blog/2020/08/taking-control-of-plot-scaling/)
- **scales** supplies the formatting, breaks and palette layer (see §1 for 1.4.0's palette classes and colour mixing). — [scales 1.4.0 post](https://tidyverse.org/blog/2025/04/scales-1-4-0/)
- **Tanaka (2025)** argues that extending ggplot2 requires knowing its internals: "to truly create any plot, knowledge of the internal system (as well as related systems like gtable and grid) is essential". The extension mechanism (replacing ggproto Coord/Facet/Geom/Guide/Layout/Position/Scale/Stat) "is typically beyond that of most users, who instead rely on extension packages". Example: ggpattern's pattern fills "would be extraordinary difficult for typical users". — [Tanaka 2025, arXiv:2510.10382](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10382)

**Gap-to-core mapping (summary table)**

| Extension | Gap filled | Status in ggplot2 (Oct 2026) | Recommendation for the new core |
|---|---|---|---|
| patchwork, cowplot | composition, shared legends and axes, figure-level annotation | not in core | **Core**: a layout algebra with collected guides and axes |
| ggrepel, geomtextpath, ggfittext | label overlap, direct labels, text fitting | not in core | **Core**: a collision-aware text mark and a direct-label transform |
| ggtext, marquee | markdown and rich text in labels and theme | marquee targets core | **Core**: rich text in every text element |
| gghighlight | emphasis, de-emphasising context | not in core | **Core**: highlight as a state, shared with interactive hover and select |
| ggh4x | nested strips, per-panel scales, panel sizes, multiple colour scales | panel sizes in 4.0 | **Core**: nested facets, per-panel scale overrides, multiple scales per aesthetic |
| ggiraph, plotly::ggplotly, animint | interactivity | not in core | **Core**: interaction aesthetics (`tooltip`, `id`, selection) plus native web output |
| gganimate | animation | not in core | Optional module (G2's enter/update/exit is the model) |
| systemfonts, ragg, textshaping | fonts, shaping, output scaling | separate packages | **Core**: font discovery, fallback, embedding, one global scaling factor |
| scales | breaks, labels, palettes, colour maths | separate package | **Core** (formatting and palette objects) |
| ggdist, ggdibbler, ggtime, ggforce | domain grammars and extra geoms | extensions | Extension API, but design for distribution-valued and time-semantic columns |

(Sources: rows summarise the findings above.)

### Inferences
- The extensions that fix *layout and text* (patchwork, cowplot, ggrepel, ggtext/marquee, ggh4x, systemfonts/ragg) solve problems that nearly every publication figure has. Their existence outside the core shows where ggplot2's architecture made these things hard: composition works on finished gtables, and text measurement belongs to the device. A new library that owns its own layout engine and text shaping can put them in core.
- Interactivity is the clearest case for "core, not extension". ggplotly has to guess interaction semantics after the fact, and ggiraph and animint show that a few interaction *aesthetics* (tooltip, data_id/key, clickSelects/showSelected) fit naturally into the grammar.
- The ggpattern/ggdist/ggtime experience suggests the extension API should make new *aesthetic types* (pattern, distribution, time granularity) and new *scale types* easy to add, not only new geoms and stats.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch individual package pages for ggrepel, gghighlight, ggforce or ggtext. Their descriptions rely on the official gallery and well-known documentation summaries, so specific feature lists (e.g. ggforce's `facet_zoom()` and `geom_mark_*()`) should be checked at https://ggforce.data-imaginist.com/ and https://yutannihilation.github.io/gghighlight/ before quoting.
- No source found giving download counts across the whole extension ecosystem, or the number of CRAN packages that depend on ggplot2.

## 3. Common pain points and critiques (theme verbosity, legends, dual axes, labels, annotations, facets, fonts, export sizing, performance, interactivity, `+` and NSE)

### Takeaway
The most-voted ggplot2 StackOverflow questions are overwhelmingly about *presentation*, not the grammar. They cover rotating axis labels, legend titles and removal, side-by-side plots, axis limits, title centring, bar ordering, dual axes, saving images, facet labels, font sizes and number formatting. Research on analysts (Pu & Kay 2023) finds the main friction is the gap between *data space* and *aesthetic space*: annotations have to be placed in data units, palettes get confused with data values, and scales cannot be inverted. Maintainers have answered piecemeal (sec_axis 2016, legend overhaul 2024, `I()` npc 2024, `theme_sub_*` 2025), while open issues on units, layer-level clipping and scale shortcuts remain.

**Dual / secondary axes**
- Hadley Wickham's answer (177 votes) said dual axes were "not possible in ggplot2 because I believe plots with separate y scales (not y-scales that are transformations of each other) are fundamentally flawed". His reasons: they are not invertible, hard to read (citing Isenberg et al.), "easily manipulated to mislead", and arbitrary ("why have only 2 scales, not 3, 4 or ten?"). — [SO answer 3101876](https://stackoverflow.com/a/3101876)
- ggplot2 2.2.0 added `sec_axis(~ . * 1.20)`, which only allows secondary axes that are *transformations* of the primary. — [SO answer 39805869](https://stackoverflow.com/a/39805869). A third answer notes "Sometimes a client wants two y scales. Giving them the 'flawed' speech is often pointless". — [SO answer 3117319](https://stackoverflow.com/a/3117319)
- lets-plot 4.10.0 (May 2026) added `ggdeck()`, which overlays independent plots "with optional shared axes for dual-axis effects". This is a pragmatic route around the same problem. — [lets-plot CHANGELOG](https://github.com/JetBrains/lets-plot/blob/master/CHANGELOG.md)

**Analyst behaviour (Pu & Kay, CHI 2023, Best Paper Honorable Mention; N=6 TidyTuesday analysts, plus a corpus of 3,649 R files from 975 contributors)** — [doi:10.1145/3544548.3580837](https://doi.org/10.1145/3544548.3580837); local copy `literature/foundations/Pu2023_analysts-use-visualization-grammar.pdf`
- Participants hard-coded values in data space to size elements, to place annotations, and to shrink dots to avoid overlap. "With no support from the grammar, participants needed to guess what data space values could achieve their desired output", and one participant expressed frustration with this "back-and-forth" process.
- A participant assigned a hex colour "#a62d3b" to the colour aesthetic. It was treated as a data value, not a colour, so the plot showed default colours. The authors call this a conceptual slip between aesthetic and data space; ggplot2's fix is `I()` or a manual scale.
- "since there are no inverse scale functions to map aesthetic space values back to the data space", placing a label "in the bottom left corner" means translating that into data values (e.g. year = 1900) and updating them whenever the data are filtered. Their quote: "analysts cannot access native units in ggplot2 unless they program custom components."
- Analysts thought in *plot types* and code-switched between the grammar and copy-pasted "plot templates". The design implication is grammar-based, editable templates.
- They quote Wickham that "theming is unimportant early on [in the development of ggplot2] but critical in the long run".
- They identify silent errors (plausible but wrong plots) as a problem and call for consistency checks between wrangling and plotting, and for "more transparency into visualization library internals—if analysts can peek into the data being plotted before and after the scale computation".

**Annotations**
- AnnoGram (2025) compared eight tools. ggplot2 and ggplot2's `annotate` primitives "reduce LOC and initial authoring effort, but weak semantic coupling still requires manual adjustments during updates". Highcharts "simplifies initial authoring but retains similar limitations". The authors argue for annotations as first-class grammar elements, with declarative targets, types and positioning, that adapt when data change. — [Rahman et al. 2025, arXiv:2507.04236](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04236)
- ggplot2 3.5.0 lets `I()` specify npc positions (see §1), which partially addresses Pu & Kay's "bottom-left corner" example. — [3.5.0 post](https://www.tidyverse.org/blog/2024/02/ggplot2-3-5-0/); local check

**Units, fonts and export sizing**
- "Due to a historical error, the unit of linewidth is roughly 0.75 mm." Text size "is measured in mm by default. This is unusual" (hence `size.unit = "pt"` and the `.pt` constant). Only "sans", "serif" and "mono" are "guaranteed to work everywhere". — [ggplot2 aesthetic specs vignette](https://ggplot2.tidyverse.org/articles/ggplot2-specs.html)
- Local check: `.pt = 2.845276` (pt per mm), and `ggsave()` defaults to `units = "in"` (also cm, mm, px) and `dpi = 300`. So a plot mixes mm (geoms), pt (theme text) and inches/pixels (output). — local check
- The scaling problem: when output size changes, point-sized text stays the same physical size. Scaling via resolution "has the added drawback that the dimensions of the plot is incorrectly encoded", so ragg added `scaling`. — [Taking control of plot scaling](https://www.tidyverse.org/blog/2020/08/taking-control-of-plot-scaling/)
- Fonts depend on the device; see systemfonts in §2. — [Fonts in R](https://tidyverse.org/blog/2025/05/fonts-in-r/)

**Performance with large data**
- Rendering cost sits in grid and the device rather than ggplot2. Build-time hotspots have included `factor()` on more than 1e6 rows and title metrics. — [profiling vignette](https://ggplot2.tidyverse.org/articles/profiling.html)
- Wickham's bin-summarise-smooth framework (2013) argues there is no point drawing more marks than pixels ("at most we have around 3,000,000 pixels"). It condenses data in O(n) to a pixel-scale summary before display, demonstrated on 76 million flights. — [Wickham 2013, bigvis paper](http://vita.had.co.nz/papers/bigvis.pdf)
- A 2015 Hacker News complaint: "My biggest problem with ggplot is that it's slow, unbearably slow". — [HN comment](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=10776989). Also an old ggplot2 mailing-list thread on slow `geom_boxplot` with about 400,000 rows. — [Google Groups](https://groups.google.com/g/ggplot2/c/7VBPbfq1AHw)

**Lack of interactivity**
- "ggplot2 doesn't provide an API for interactive features". — [plotly-r.com](https://plotly-r.com/improving-ggplotly.html). The ecosystem's answers (ggiraph SVG plus JS, plotly conversion, animint compiler plus renderer) are all bolt-ons; see §2.

**The `+` operator and non-standard evaluation**
- A blogger's account of a 2019 LondonR talk reports Wickham framing ggplot2's `+` as a legacy mistake: "the magrittr pipe `%>%` was not in use when ggplot2 was written". The same talk reportedly listed "tidyeval pushed too early" among tidyverse mistakes. (This is a secondary report, Martin Chan, Aug 2019.) — [R-bloggers](https://www.r-bloggers.com/2019/08/londonr-hadley-wickham-tidyverses-greatest-hits/)
- `+` dispatch problems drove part of the S7 migration ([#3986](https://github.com/tidyverse/ggplot2/issues/3986), [#5536](https://github.com/tidyverse/ggplot2/issues/5536)), and 4.0 produced new `Ops.S7_object`/`+.gg` conflicts. — [Posit Community](https://forum.posit.co/t/incompatible-methods-ops-s7-object-gg-for/207470)
- NSE costs show up in the top questions as "no visible binding" (226 votes) and "use a variable to specify a column name" (218). — [SO 9439256](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/9439256), [SO 22309285](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/22309285)
- Kay (2024) on the other side of the trade-off: Vega-Lite's JSON is "a horrifying mess of visual noise", and its JS API "without R's facility for capturing and re-writing abstract syntax trees … doesn't quite reach the succinctness of ggplot2. This seems to be a fundamental limitation when writing domain-specific languages in JavaScript". — [Kay 2024, footnote 6](https://mucollective.northwestern.edu/files/2023-ggdist.pdf)
- Learning curve: ggplot2's component-named API (`scale_*`, `geom_*`) "allows for greater expressive capability … but introduces a steeper learning curve". `qplot()` was deprecated, and "Most ggplot2 users likely do not comprehend the entire system". — [Tanaka 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10382)

### Inferences
- The top StackOverflow themes map onto a short list of *defaults and shortcuts* a new library should provide: axis-label rotation and overlap avoidance by default; a one-argument way to rename or hide any legend or axis; ordering categories by value; percentage and number formatting presets; title alignment as a theme choice; a single save call with physical units; and composition with shared legends. Each of these took one or more extensions or 50-line `theme()` calls in ggplot2.
- The data-space/aesthetic-space problem argues for **mixed coordinate systems for every position-like parameter**: data units, panel-relative npc, absolute pt/px offsets and combinations of them (e.g. `x = data(1950) + pt(4)`). It also argues for **invertible scales** exposed to users, so a click, drag or "corner" can be mapped back to data. Interactive zoom and brush need scale inversion anyway.
- Julia offers a middle path on NSE. Macros or `Symbol`/`Pair` column references (`:x => "Label"`) avoid R's "no visible binding" problem while keeping succinct column references. A pipe-friendly or functional composition (`plot |> layer(...)`, or `*`/`+` with documented algebra, as in AlgebraOfGraphics, which another researcher covers) avoids ggplot2's operator-dispatch pitfalls if it is defined on plain immutable values.
- Dual axes: offer `sec_axis`-style transformed secondary axes as safe by default, plus an explicit, clearly named opt-in for independent overlays (like lets-plot's `ggdeck()`), because clients ask for them.

## 4. Other grammar implementations: APIs, layer composition, scales and themes, interactivity

### Takeaway
Post-2020 grammar libraries keep the core of marks plus encodings plus scales, and differ in four ways. (1) *Data binding*: per-mark data (Observable Plot, TanStack Charts) versus one plot-level data frame (ggplot2, plotnine, lets-plot). (2) *Composition syntax*: `+` (ggplot2, plotnine, lets-plot), method chaining on immutable objects (seaborn.objects), arrays of marks (Observable Plot), or nested specs and chained APIs (G2). (3) *Styling*: a theme object (ggplot2 family) versus CSS (Observable Plot) or rcParams (seaborn). (4) *Interactivity*, which ranges from none (ggplot2, plotnine) through pointer and tooltip only (Observable Plot) and tooltips plus pan/zoom (lets-plot) to a full interaction and animation grammar (AntV G2). G2 is the closest existing model for the goal of a "grammar plus Highcharts-style interaction".

### Cited Findings

**AntV G2 v5 (JavaScript; Ant Group)**
- Positioning: a "concise and progressive grammar" with these core concepts: **Mark, Transform, Scale, Coordinate, Composition, Animation, Interaction**. The current version is 5.4.8, released 2026-01-06. — [What is G2](https://g2.antv.antgroup.com/en/manual/introduction/what-is-g2); [G2 releases](https://github.com/antvis/G2/releases)
- Paper: Su, Wang, Lin, Liao, Zhou, Zhu & Chen, "G2: A customizable web-based framework for authoring interactive visualizations", *Visual Informatics* 10 (Sept 2026), 100307. It "employs a data-driven grammar of graphics and defines a uniform set of interaction specifications". Since March 2016 G2 has had "360 iterations, received 12,000 stars, and supported over 22,600 related projects". — [doi:10.1016/j.visinf.2026.100307](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.visinf.2026.100307) (abstract via [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2468502X26000033); full text not accessible to me)
- Interaction is declared in the spec at the view or mark level (`interaction: { tooltip: {}, brushHighlight: {} }`) and can be switched off with `false`. Mark-level settings override view-level ones ("Interaction has a bubbling nature"). Built-ins include tooltip, elementHighlight (and by-X and by-colour variants), elementSelect, brushHighlight/brushFilter (and X/Y and axis variants), legendFilter/legendHighlight, sliderFilter/scrollbarFilter, chartIndex, fisheye and elementHoverScale. — [G2 interaction overview](https://g2.antv.antgroup.com/en/manual/core/interaction/overview); [G2 docs index](https://g2.antv.antgroup.com/llms.txt)
- **State styling**: marks declare `state: { selected: {...}, unselected: {...}, active: {...}, inactive: {...} }`, which the interactions consume. Interactions emit events (`chart.on('brush:filter', ...)`) and can be triggered programmatically (`chart.emit(...)`). Custom interactions are registered with `register('interaction.name', ...)`. — [interaction overview](https://g2.antv.antgroup.com/en/manual/core/interaction/overview)
- **Animation grammar**: enter/update/exit states; data-driven duration and delay; morphing, fade and scale transitions; easing and timing; and a `timingKeyframe` composition for keyframe storytelling. — [G2 animation overview](https://g2.antv.antgroup.com/en/manual/core/animate/overview); [docs index](https://g2.antv.antgroup.com/llms.txt)
- **Composition**: `view`, `spaceLayer`, `spaceFlex`, `facetRect`, `facetCircle`, `repeatMatrix` and `timingKeyframe`, with children nested in the spec. — [docs index](https://g2.antv.antgroup.com/llms.txt)
- **Rendering**: built on AntV G, which supports "Canvas, SVG, and WebGL, with Canvas renderer being the built-in default". Only Canvas ships in the core bundle. — [G2 renderer plugin docs](https://g2.antv.antgroup.com/en/manual/extra-topics/plugin/renderer)
- **Themes**: `light` (default), `dark`, `classic`, `classicDark` and `academy` ("Suitable for academic papers, reports"). Themes can be set at view or mark level, and the mark level wins. — [G2 theme overview](https://g2.antv.antgroup.com/en/manual/core/theme/overview)

**Observable Plot (JavaScript; Bostock et al.)**
- "featuring scales and layered marks in the grammar of graphics style". Its goal is "to help you get a meaningful visualization quickly to accelerate analysis". — [What is Plot?](https://observablehq.com/plot/what-is-plot)
- **Departures from ggplot2**: "Each mark supplies its own tabular data", with no single plot data frame ([Plots](https://observablehq.com/plot/features/plots)). Transforms are *option-wrapping functions* (`Plot.rectY(data, Plot.binX({y: "count"}, {x: "weight", fill: "sex"}))`), and many marks apply *implicit* transforms ("the rectY mark applies an implicit stackY transform") ([Transforms](https://observablehq.com/plot/features/transforms)). Facets are mark channels (`fx`, `fy`) ([Facets](https://observablehq.com/plot/features/facets)). Axes, grids and frames are *marks*, generated implicitly if not declared ([Scales](https://observablehq.com/plot/features/scales)). Some marks require scale types (e.g. barY requires a band x scale). Any channel can opt out of scaling with `{value, scale}` or the identity type.
- **Styling** goes through a `style` option (inline CSS) and `className` rather than a theme object. "By default, the returned plot has a max-width of 100%, and the system-ui font. Plot's marks and axes default to currentColor." The default width is 640 px, and the height is inferred from the scales. The default continuous colour scheme is *turbo*, "chosen primarily to ensure high-contrast visibility". — [Plots](https://observablehq.com/plot/features/plots); [Scales](https://observablehq.com/plot/features/scales)
- **Interactivity**: a pointer transform (since 0.6.7) "filters a mark interactively such that only the point closest to the pointer is rendered", plus a `tip` mark (`tip: true`) and a crosshair mark. Brushing and selection are "under development" (issue #5), panning and zooming are "planned" (#1590), and declarative animation is "planned" (#166). — [Interactions](https://observablehq.com/plot/features/interactions); [Pointer](https://observablehq.com/plot/interactions/pointer)
- Release cadence: the latest tag is v0.6.17 (Feb 2025), although commits continued into Sept 2026. — [GitHub releases](https://github.com/observablehq/plot/releases)

**lets-plot (JetBrains; Python/Kotlin) and Kandy (Kotlin)**
- Described as "A faithful port of R's ggplot2 to Python and Kotlin", with multiplatform rendering (notebooks, IDEs, Compose Multiplatform). — [lets-plot.org](https://lets-plot.org/)
- **Interactivity is built in.** Per-layer tooltips are set via `tooltips=layer_tooltips()` ([tooltips docs](https://lets-plot.org/python/pages/tooltips.html)). `ggtb()` adds a toolbar with "pan, rubber-band zoom, and center-point zoom", and the mouse wheel zooms with any tool ([ggtb](https://lets-plot.org/kotlin/api-reference/-lets--plot--kotlin/org.jetbrains.letsPlot.interact/ggtb.html)). Recent releases: 4.8.0 (2025-11) added `ggtb(size_zoomin, size_basis)`, grouped and multi-layer tooltips, and shared legends in `gggrid(guides=)`; 4.9.0 (2026-03) propagates pan and zoom across `gggrid()` subplots with shared axes; 4.10.0 (2026-05) added `ggdeck()` overlays; 4.11.0 (2026-06-30, the newest) added `tooltip_merge`/`tooltip_max_count` and text halos (`halo_width`/`halo_color`). — [CHANGELOG](https://github.com/JetBrains/lets-plot/blob/master/CHANGELOG.md)
- **Default theme**: `theme_minimal2()` is "the default theme similar to theme_minimal() adding an x-axis line and only major gridlines". Colour "flavors" (e.g. `flavor_standard()`) sit on top of themes. — [lets-plot theme source](https://github.com/JetBrains/lets-plot/blob/master/python-package/lets_plot/plot/theme_set.py); [CHANGELOG](https://github.com/JetBrains/lets-plot/blob/master/CHANGELOG.md)
- **Kandy** (Dec 2023) is a Kotlin DSL "inspired by The Grammar of Graphics" with a backend-neutral "Plot Intermediate Representation" and two backends, kandy-lets-plot and kandy-echarts. — [JetBrains blog](https://blog.jetbrains.com/kotlin/2023/12/kandy-the-new-kotlin-plotting-library-by-jetbrains/); [Kandy GitHub](https://github.com/Kotlin/kandy)

**plotnine (Python, on matplotlib)**
- A ggplot2 clone that uses `+`. v0.15.0 added "plot composition using intuitive algebra" (`|`, `/`), plot tags and `plot_title_position`. v0.14 added a layout manager "eliminating manual spacing adjustments". The newest release is 0.15.8 (2026-08-14). It has no native interactivity; output goes through matplotlib. — [plotnine changelog](https://plotnine.org/changelog.html)

**seaborn.objects (Python, 2022; seaborn v0.12+)**
- A `Plot` object with method chaining (`.add`, `.facet`, `.pair`, `.scale`, `.label`, `.limit`, `.share`, `.layout`, `.theme`, `.on`). Methods "return cloned objects rather than modifying in place". It "more cleanly separates representation and transformation, allowing you to compose Mark and Stat objects", plus Move objects (Dodge, Jitter) for position adjustments. `.scale()` accepts shorthands (`y="log"`, `color="flare"`, `pointsize=(2,10)`), `.theme()` takes matplotlib rcParams, and `.on()` targets matplotlib Figures or SubFigures. Its status: "currently experimental and incomplete". — [objects interface tutorial](https://seaborn.pydata.org/tutorial/objects_interface.html)
- The latest seaborn release is v0.13.2 (Jan 2024), although commits continue (Oct 2026). — [seaborn releases](https://github.com/mwaskom/seaborn/releases). JOSS paper (pre-objects): [Waskom 2021](https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.03021)

**Bokeh (Python; `bokeh.plotting`)** (a glyph and tool model, not strictly a grammar)
- Interactive legends: "clicking or tapping on the legend entries will hide or mute the corresponding glyph", via `click_policy = "hide" | "mute"` (mute uses `muted_alpha`/`muted_color`). The limitation is that "Interactive legends only work on 'per-glyph' legends. Grouped legends do not yet support" it (Bokeh 3.10.0). — [Bokeh legends guide](https://docs.bokeh.org/en/latest/docs/user_guide/interaction/legends.html)

**Swift Charts (Apple, 2022)**
- A declarative, mark-based framework (`BarMark`, `LineMark`, `PointMark`, `AreaMark`, `RectangleMark`, `RuleMark`). `.foregroundStyle(by: .value("Type", …))` encodes a series and automatically generates a legend. Selection APIs such as `chartXSelection` arrived in iOS 17. — [WWDC22 "Swift Charts: Raise the bar"](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10137/); [tutorial on Charts interactivity](https://antongubarenko.substack.com/p/swiftui-charts-interactivity-part)

**TanStack Charts (TypeScript, July 2026)**
- "A tiny TypeScript visualization grammar for responsive, accessible, server-rendered charts—powered by granular D3 primitives" (repo created 2026-07-28). Its grammar is Data, Marks, Channels, Scales, Guides and Layers. Charts compile to "a renderer-neutral scene" consumed by a built-in SVG renderer or custom renderers. "A chart is not a special-purpose component with a fixed series model": each mark gets its own data iterable. — [TanStack Charts: Grammar of Graphics](https://tanstack.com/charts/latest/docs/concepts/grammar-of-graphics); [GitHub TanStack/charts](https://github.com/TanStack/charts)

**Rust and other languages (2022–2026)**
- **ggplot-rs** (created Feb 2026) is a ggplot2 re-implementation with a dependency-free SVG backend or a plotters backend. Its stats are validated against ggplot2 4.0.3's `ggplot_build()`. Input can be plain vectors, Polars or Arrow RecordBatches (e.g. from DuckDB). — [ggplot-rs](https://github.com/sipemu/ggplot-rs)
- **Charton** (created Dec 2025, about 440 stars) offers "Altair-style declarative plotting for Rust", Polars-friendly and Wasm-ready, with a Nushell plugin. — [Charton](https://github.com/wangjiawen2013/charton)
- **kuva** (created May 2025, about 880 stars) is a *typology*-style scientific plotting library ("64 plot types", SVG, optional PNG/PDF, and a CLI that renders in the terminal). It is a useful contrast to grammar designs. — [kuva](https://github.com/Psy-Fer/kuva)
- **ggplotnim** (Nim) is a ggplot2 port still maintained in 2026. — [ggplotnim](https://github.com/Vindaar/ggplotnim)
- **gramm** (MATLAB, JOSS 2018) is "Inspired by ggplot2" and brings grouping, faceting and stats to MATLAB's low-level plotting. — [Morel 2018](https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.00568)

### Inferences
- G2 shows that a grammar can carry Highcharts-class interaction without leaving the declarative model. Its key ingredients are a small set of named interactions configured per view or mark, mark **states** (active/inactive/selected) as styling targets, an event bus for programmatic control, and an animation grammar with enter/update/exit. A Julia design could adopt the same vocabulary and map it to a web backend.
- Observable Plot's per-mark data and option-wrapping transforms make layered annotation (each annotation layer with its own small table) and transform composition very natural. Its implicit axis and grid marks unify "guides" and "marks", so axes can be styled with the same machinery. The trade-off is that the lack of a theme object pushes styling into CSS, which works for the web but is weak for PDF and publication output.
- lets-plot and plotnine show that a faithful ggplot2 API is portable, and the ecosystem expects it: users and LLM agents know ggplot2 idioms. Being familiar to ggplot2 users has adoption value even where the new design departs from it.
- Typology libraries (kuva) and the "plot templates" finding (Pu & Kay) suggest providing high-level chart functions *built on* the grammar that return editable grammar objects. This is the opposite of the deprecated `qplot()`, which was a separate shortcut.
- A renderer-neutral intermediate scene (TanStack Charts, G2-on-G, animint's compiler/renderer split, Kandy's Plot IR) is the common architecture behind multi-backend output, both static vector for print and interactive HTML. The new Julia library should compile to such a scene graph so that SVG, PDF, PNG and an interactive JS runtime are all just backends.

## 5. Default-theme choices that make output look professional or unprofessional

### Takeaway
ggplot2's grey-panel default is deliberate and argued for, but it is the single most-overridden default. The ecosystem's answers converge on light, minimal themes (white paper, few or no vertical gridlines, an axis line on x), semantic colour roles, and typographic control (font family pairs, base size). Libraries designed later default to minimal styling: lets-plot's `theme_minimal2`, Observable Plot's system-ui font with currentColor and no default grid, and G2's light theme plus a separate `academy` theme. ggplot2 4.0's ink/paper/accent and palette-in-theme make whole-plot restyling a one-liner.

### Cited Findings
- ggplot2's rationale for its default: "The grey background gives the plot a similar typographic colour to the text, ensuring that the graphics fit in with the flow of a document without jumping out with a bright white background", and it "creates a continuous field of colour which ensures that the plot is perceived as a single visual entity". Gridlines "have little visual impact and we can easily 'tune' them out". — [ggplot2 book, Themes](https://ggplot2-book.org/themes.html)
- Local check: `theme_grey()` uses panel fill `#EBEBEB`, white gridlines and base text size 11. The default discrete hue palette starts `#F8766D, #00BA38, #619CFF`. — local check
- Demand to undo defaults shows up in SO questions: "Remove grid, background color, and top and right borders" (159), "Emulate ggplot2 default color palette" (276), "Center plot title" (409), and "ggplot2 plot without axes, legends, etc" (193). — [Stack Exchange API pull](https://api.stackexchange.com/2.3/questions?order=desc&sort=votes&tagged=ggplot2&site=stackoverflow)
- cowplot originally *replaced* the default theme on load and reversed this in 1.0.0 (2019). Opinionated defaults need to be explicit and opt-in once a library has users. — [cowplot NEWS](https://wilkelab.org/cowplot/news/index.html)
- ggplot2 4.0: complete themes accept `ink`, `paper`, `accent`, `base_size` and `header_family`/`base_family` (font pairing). A light ink on dark paper gives dark mode. Custom themes should be written as functions that call base themes, so that "when ggplot2 adds new elements … your theme also remains complete". — [ggplot2 styling post](https://tidyverse.org/blog/2025/10/ggplot2-styling/)
- lets-plot's default `theme_minimal2()` is minimal plus an x-axis line and major gridlines only. — [lets-plot source](https://github.com/JetBrains/lets-plot/blob/master/python-package/lets_plot/plot/theme_set.py)
- Observable Plot's defaults: system-ui font, currentColor for marks and axes (it inherits the page's text colour, so it adapts to dark mode), 640 px width, grid off unless `grid: true`, and turbo for continuous colour "to ensure high-contrast visibility". — [Plots](https://observablehq.com/plot/features/plots); [Scales](https://observablehq.com/plot/features/scales); [What is Plot?](https://observablehq.com/plot/what-is-plot) (the faceted example adds `grid: true` explicitly)
- G2 ships `light` (default), `dark`, `classic`, `classicDark` and an `academy` theme "Suitable for academic papers, reports". — [G2 themes](https://g2.antv.antgroup.com/en/manual/core/theme/overview)
- Typography is where R output most often looks amateur. Only "sans/serif/mono" are portable, and choosing weights or widths needs systemfonts `register_variant()`. — [ggplot2 specs](https://ggplot2.tidyverse.org/articles/ggplot2-specs.html); [Fonts in R](https://tidyverse.org/blog/2025/05/fonts-in-r/)
- Gramm's JOSS paper compares default MATLAB output with gramm output to argue that grammar-driven defaults (grouping colours, confidence bands) improve plots. — [Morel 2018](https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.00568)

### Inferences
- Defaults that read as "professional": white or paper-coloured background; horizontal-only major gridlines in a light tint of ink; an axis line on the baseline; left-aligned title and subtitle hierarchy (ggplot2 already left-aligns titles, against user expectation; offer `plot_title_position = "plot"`-style alignment to the full figure); a real font family with a heading/body pair; tabular numerals for tick labels; a colour-blind-safe categorical palette defined in the theme; legends placed top-left or as direct labels for few series. (The palette, colour and typography specifics are covered by the design researcher. These are inferences from the cited defaults and user overrides.)
- Defaults that read as "unprofessional": grey panel fills, heavy borders, right-side legends that steal width, mm-based text sizes that drift when output size changes, overlapping tick labels, and default hue palettes of equal luminance (poor in greyscale; this is my inference, not verified here).
- A good default system needs **physical units first** (pt for text and lines, inches or cm for figure size) plus a single scaling factor (ragg's lesson), so that a theme looks the same at journal column width and on a slide.

### Gaps
- I found no empirical study rating perceived professionalism of default themes across libraries. This section rests on design rationales, defaults and user override patterns.
- I did not retrieve plotnine's or seaborn.objects' exact default theme parameters.

## 6. Synthesis: what a new grammar should build into its core, and what it should do differently

### Takeaway
Combining §§1–5, the evidence points to a core that includes composition, text, annotation, interaction and output sizing, which ggplot2 leaves to extensions. The architecture should be designed around a public, renderer-neutral "built plot" intermediate representation, typed extension points and a semantic theme.

### Cited Findings
(Each lesson cites the evidence from the earlier sections.)
1. **Publish a stable intermediate representation.** ggplot2's build/gtable split is what downstream tools rely on ([Internals](https://ggplot2-book.org/internals.html); ggplotly [plotly-r.com](https://plotly-r.com/improving-ggplotly.html); ggplot-rs validation [repo](https://github.com/sipemu/ggplot-rs)). Analysts want to see data "before and after the scale computation" ([Pu & Kay 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3544548.3580837)).
2. **Compile to a renderer-neutral scene** with SVG, PDF, PNG and interactive-web backends (TanStack Charts' "renderer-neutral scene" [docs](https://tanstack.com/charts/latest/docs/concepts/grammar-of-graphics); G2 on Canvas/SVG/WebGL [docs](https://g2.antv.antgroup.com/en/manual/extra-topics/plugin/renderer); animint's compiler/renderer split [Sievert et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1080/10618600.2018.1513367); Kandy's Plot IR [blog](https://blog.jetbrains.com/kotlin/2023/12/kandy-the-new-kotlin-plotting-library-by-jetbrains/)).
3. **Make interaction part of the grammar**: tooltip/id/selection aesthetics ([ggiraph](https://www.ardata.fr/ggiraph-book/intro.html), [animint](http://tdhock.github.io/assets/papers/Sievert2019jcgs.pdf)); named interactions per view or mark with mark states and events ([G2](https://g2.antv.antgroup.com/en/manual/core/interaction/overview)); pointer, tip and crosshair marks ([Observable Plot](https://observablehq.com/plot/features/interactions)); toolbar pan and zoom shared across composed subplots ([lets-plot 4.9.0](https://github.com/JetBrains/lets-plot/blob/master/CHANGELOG.md)); legend toggle hide/mute ([Bokeh](https://docs.bokeh.org/en/latest/docs/user_guide/interaction/legends.html)). Avoid post-hoc conversion ([plotly-r.com](https://plotly-r.com/improving-ggplotly.html)).
4. **Use a semantic, single style tree from day one**: ink/paper/accent roles, palettes in the theme, layer defaults in the theme, and shortcut sub-themes. ggplot2 got these only in 2025, after an issue open since 2017 ([4.0.0](https://tidyverse.org/blog/2025/09/ggplot2-4-0-0/); [#2239](https://github.com/tidyverse/ggplot2/issues/2239)). Wickham: theming is "critical in the long run" (quoted in [Pu & Kay 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3544548.3580837)).
5. **Composition in core**: grids and insets with collected legends and axes ([patchwork 1.2.0](https://www.data-imaginist.com/posts/2024-01-05-patchwork-1-2-0/); SO side-by-side 459 votes, common legend 215).
6. **Text in core**: rich text and markdown everywhere ([marquee](https://tidyverse.org/blog/2024/05/marquee-0-1-0/); [#2743](https://github.com/tidyverse/ggplot2/issues/2743)); collision-aware labels and direct labelling (ggrepel; SO "Label points" 249; [Pu & Kay](https://doi.org/10.1145/3544548.3580837)); font discovery, fallback and shaping ([Fonts in R](https://tidyverse.org/blog/2025/05/fonts-in-r/)).
7. **Annotations as first-class grammar objects** positioned in mixed units (data, npc and absolute) and anchored to data or marks ([AnnoGram](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04236); [#5609](https://github.com/tidyverse/ggplot2/issues/5609); [Pu & Kay](https://doi.org/10.1145/3544548.3580837)), with layer-level clipping ([#6773](https://github.com/tidyverse/ggplot2/issues/6773)).
8. **One unit system plus one scaling factor**: avoid ggplot2's mm/pt/inch mix and the "historical error" in linewidth ([specs](https://ggplot2.tidyverse.org/articles/ggplot2-specs.html)), and offer ragg-style `scaling` ([post](https://www.tidyverse.org/blog/2020/08/taking-control-of-plot-scaling/)).
9. **Rich facets**: nested strips, per-panel scale overrides, explicit panel sizes, and layer-to-panel assignment ([ggh4x](https://teunbrand.github.io/ggh4x/); 4.0's `panel.widths` and `layer(layout=)` [post](https://tidyverse.org/blog/2025/09/ggplot2-4-0-0/)).
10. **Scale shortcuts and a label dictionary**: set breaks, limits, expansion and labels without a full scale call ([#4269](https://github.com/tidyverse/ggplot2/issues/4269)); a label precedence chain with data dictionaries and column metadata ([4.0.0](https://tidyverse.org/blog/2025/09/ggplot2-4-0-0/)).
11. **Large data by design**: stats that bin and summarise to pixel scale ([Wickham 2013](http://vita.had.co.nz/papers/bigvis.pdf)), plus a backend able to draw millions of marks (ggplot2's bottleneck is grid and the device: [profiling](https://ggplot2.tidyverse.org/articles/profiling.html)).
12. **Typed, documented extension points with validation**: avoid the S3→ggproto→S7 retrofits and the revdep breakage ([#6352](https://github.com/tidyverse/ggplot2/issues/6352), [#6505](https://github.com/tidyverse/ggplot2/issues/6505), [#6507](https://github.com/tidyverse/ggplot2/issues/6507)). Make new aesthetic and scale types extensible, as ggdist, ggdibbler and ggtime needed ([Kay 2024](https://doi.org/10.1109/TVCG.2023.3327195); [Mason 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24217); [Huang 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25656)).
13. **Composition syntax on immutable values** (seaborn.objects' clone-on-method [docs](https://seaborn.pydata.org/tutorial/objects_interface.html)). Avoid operator-dispatch fragility ([#3986](https://github.com/tidyverse/ggplot2/issues/3986); [Posit Community](https://forum.posit.co/t/incompatible-methods-ops-s7-object-gg-for/207470)) and the NSE costs ([SO 9439256](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/9439256), [SO 22309285](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/22309285)).
14. **Templates built on the grammar**, returning editable grammar objects ([Pu & Kay](https://doi.org/10.1145/3544548.3580837); `qplot()` deprecation per [Tanaka 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10382)).
15. **Secondary axes**: transformed by default, with independent overlays as an explicit opt-in ([Wickham SO answer](https://stackoverflow.com/a/3101876); lets-plot `ggdeck()` [CHANGELOG](https://github.com/JetBrains/lets-plot/blob/master/CHANGELOG.md)).
16. **Minimal, typographically considered defaults** with a separate publication ("academy") preset ([G2 themes](https://g2.antv.antgroup.com/en/manual/core/theme/overview); [lets-plot theme_minimal2](https://github.com/JetBrains/lets-plot/blob/master/python-package/lets_plot/plot/theme_set.py)).

### Inferences
- Several of these (2, 3, 7, 8, 11) are architectural and cannot be added later without breaking changes. ggplot2's decade of retrofits shows this. They should drive the choice of rendering backend and of the intermediate representation before any marks are written.
- Items 5, 6, 9 and 10 are mostly API surface and can be added incrementally, but they should be on the first-release checklist because they dominate real-world questions.

### Gaps
- No user study directly compares ggplot2 with an "interaction-native" grammar (G2, lets-plot) on the authoring effort for publication-plus-interactive output. The recommendations are evidence-informed, not validated.
