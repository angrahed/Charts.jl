## What a chart owes every reader

Most charting libraries treat accessibility as an add-on. Highcharts' accessibility module is not loaded by default, although Highsoft recommends always including it. Of 57 JSON visualisation languages, only four (Highcharts, Vega-Lite, ECharts and FusionCharts) provide accessibility features of their own (McNutt 2022). Datawrapper sets the standard for what should happen without being asked: text that stays readable on any background, a check for colour blindness, screen-reader descriptions such as "Line chart with 7 lines", and navigation with the arrow keys.

The research gives a target. Zong and colleagues describe accessible charts along three dimensions, tested with 13 blind and low-vision readers: structure, how a chart's parts are organised for traversal; navigation, how a reader moves through them; and description, what is read out (Zong et al. 2022). Lundgard and Satyanarayan define four levels of description: the chart's construction (level 1), its statistics (2), the trends a sighted reader perceives (3), and context and interpretation (4). Blind readers found levels 2 and 3 most useful, and 63 per cent objected to the author's subjective interpretation (Lundgard & Satyanarayan 2022). Chartability lists ten critical heuristics, among them low contrast, small text, interaction that works with only one kind of input, and a chart with no title, summary, caption or table (Elavsky et al. 2022).

## Generated from the specification

A grammar already holds what an accessible chart needs: what is plotted against what, in which groups and on which scales. Olli turns Vega-Lite and Observable Plot specifications into a tree a screen reader can navigate (Blanco et al. 2022). Umwelt drives visual, textual and sonified views from one data model, with "a shared query predicate that is reified across all modalities" (Zong et al. 2024). Data Navigator builds the same kind of structure over raster and WebGL renderers (Elavsky et al. 2024). {{pkg}} compiles accessibility the way it compiles the scene ({{fig a11y}}).

<!-- figure: a11y -->

- **A tree.** The chart, its facets, its axes and legends, intervals along an axis and the data points, with lateral links between corresponding parts of different facets. It becomes an ARIA tree in HTML and structure in SVG, and it works over Canvas and WebGL layers because it does not depend on the drawn marks.
- **Descriptions.** Level 1 is generated from the specification ("Line chart of real GDP growth by year, 2010 to 2026, for four regions"). Level 2 is computed in Julia from the data and the statistics: ranges, extremes, the last value of each series. Level 3 summaries of trends are optional and marked as heuristic. Level 4 is left to the author.
- **Keys.** Every Tier 0 behaviour has a keyboard binding ({{tab fallbacks}}). That is straightforward because selections are decoupled from the events that set them.
- **Announcements.** Changes of legend, filter and drilldown are announced through live regions.
- **A table.** The data behind the chart are always available as a table.
- **Sound.** Sonification is one more renderer of the same scene, mapping position to time and value to pitch, after the Erie grammar (Kim et al. 2024).

The open question is quality. No study has evaluated level 2 descriptions generated by a grammar's compiler, and accessible zoom and brushing for screen-reader users are barely researched. {{pkg}} will test its descriptions with blind and low-vision readers before calling them done.

## Checked at build time

The rules engine that sets the defaults also checks the result. `lint(c)` runs the checks in {{tab lint}} and reports each failure with the element at fault and a suggested fix. A house style can make any check an error.

| Check | Threshold | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Text contrast | 4.5:1 against the background | WCAG 1.4.3 |
| Graphical contrast | 3:1 for the lines, points and bars needed to read the chart | WCAG 1.4.11 |
| Adjacent areas | 3:1 between neighbouring slices or stacks, or separators, or labels | WCAG 1.4.11 |
| Colour-vision deficiency | categorical colours stay apart under simulated protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia | Machado et al. 2009 |
| Greyscale | categorical colours differ in lightness, or shapes or patterns repeat the encoding | Tol 2021; Datawrapper |
| Text size | at least 12 px on the web and 6 pt in print | Datawrapper; Nature |
| Colour alone | a colour encoding with no label, shape or pattern is flagged | Chartability |
| Missing text | no title, no source or no description | Chartability |
| Too many colours | more than six categories | house styles |
| Truncated bars | a bar axis that does not include zero | Correll et al. 2020 |
| Rotated labels | a rotated tick label, with a better choice of ticks or format suggested | {{§ axes-ticks-and-numbers}} |
| Motion | animation without a pause control or reduced-motion support | WCAG 2.2.2 |
